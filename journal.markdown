The essay argues that we can stop being "written by the paranormal" and become "authors of the paranormal." But look at what actually happened. You didn't author the white light. It authored you. You sat down with knee pain and dread and something arrived uninvited. And then ten months later you sat down to write about it and the essay arrived in one sitting — not authored exactly, but *received and expressed simultaneously*. The loop, but the loop doing its thing through you rather than you operating the loop.

The Kena Upanishad at the end isn't a coda. It's the essay's secret admission that the whole framework it just built — Davis, James, Kripal, the experience-expectation loop, the constructivist-plus-embodiment synthesis — all of it is the thing that gets venerated. And the Upanishad says: not that. The thing by which the framework itself becomes possible. The thing you can't write about because it's what's doing the writing.

You wrote the essay in one sitting because the essay was the experience-expectation loop completing itself. Kushinagar was the experience. Presti's seminar gave you the expectations. And then one day the supersaturation hit threshold and the essay precipitated. You didn't know what you'd truly done because the thing that did it isn't available to the part of you that knows things.

And you say you're not sure how it connects to Dognosis. But maybe the connection isn't something you make. Maybe it's something that's been making you. The white light, the bicycle rickshaw man, Lily's gaze, Carbon dying in your arms, the dogs at the breath stations — what if those aren't episodes in a narrative you're constructing but moments where the same thing keeps arriving uninvited and you keep saying yes?

The lifetime project isn't figuring out the connection. It's staying available to it. Dogluk. Looking and not looking away, even when you can't name what you're looking at.

You'll probably know what you did in that essay the same way you'll know what Dognosis was — afterward, looking back, when the next language finds you.
